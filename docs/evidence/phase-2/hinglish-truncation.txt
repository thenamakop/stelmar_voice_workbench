Cause, from the engine's own warnings on those three clips (a diagnostic decode of each, same settings; the engine's segment_dropped warning now carries the guard's numbers, no text)
The engine's guard (PLAN section 6: drop a segment whose compression ratio is over 2.4) removed whole chunks:
| clip | chunk (ms) | compression ratio | avg log-prob | words | what it was |
| audio_1444 | 28,990 to 42,830 | 2.78 (2.53 without context) | -0.02 | 38 | real speech: a sentence said twice. whisper-cli on the same chunk gives the same text and ratio 2.53; the reference text of the whole clip itself has ratio 2.43 |
| audio_908 | 25,050 to 32,610 | 4.23 | -0.21 | 26 | repetition loop (many short words) |
| audio_908 | 32,610 to 46,730 | 5.45, hit the token limit | -0.27 | 56 | repetition loop |
| audio_37 | 90 to 13,350 | 10.00, hit the token limit, ended inside a character | -0.13 | 37 | repetition loop |
So there are two separate defects:
1. The 2.4 threshold comes from English text. Devanagari costs three UTF-8 bytes per character with two of them nearly constant, so ordinary Hindi, and especially Hinglish, compresses better than English; legitimate speech goes over 2.4 (audio_1444). That is a Latin-only shortcut in a guard (AGENTS.md non-negotiable 6).
2. Genuine loops (ratio 4 to 10) are dropped instead of retried, so the speech in that chunk is lost. whisper-cli's 5a run never showed loops because its previous-text prompt was corrupted on Windows (every Devanagari character became "?", see wer-comparison.txt), so the loops appear only with correct context.

Not decided here: PLAN section 6 says "drop a segment ... when the compression ratio is over 2.4"; changing the guard (script-neutral ratio, and a retry before dropping) changes a PLAN decision and is for the owner to approve.
