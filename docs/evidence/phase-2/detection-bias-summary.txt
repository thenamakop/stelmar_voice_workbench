Reading (what the numbers say, and what they do not):
- Pure English, with an Indian accent or not, is almost never taken for Hindi: 7 of 629 chunks (1.1%, wrong-detection probability 0.67 to 0.74, none over 0.9) for the accented speakers, 0 of 147 for FLEURS English. So the stock medium restricted to en and hi does not have a general accent problem.
- Pure Hindi is never taken for English (0 of 157).
- In Hindi-matrix code-switched utterances 17.0% of chunks are detected as English, 67 of them with probability over 0.9. The set label is the utterance's main language, so many of these chunks are English-led and detecting English there is not an error; this number measures how often the language flips between chunks, not how often detection is wrong. It cannot be read as a wrong-detection rate.
- Together with the hand-labelled baseline (codeswitch-labels.txt: 4 of the 5 English-led chunks in Hinglish speech detected as Hindi, probability 0.68 to 0.98), this points to the difficulty being English words inside code-switched speech, not Indian-accented English as such. Hypothesis only: the sample of labelled chunks is 10 and one speaker set.
- The most likely language among all of whisper's was neither English nor Hindi in 15 of 629, 11 of 157 and 18 of 724 chunks (2.4%, 7.0%, 2.5%): short chunks and non-speech, restricted away by the en,hi list.
- Svarah (added 11 Nov, after the owner accepted its terms; licence CC BY 4.0 per its README, evaluation use fine): 2 of 94 chunks (2.1%) detected as Hindi, wrong-detection probability 0.67 to 0.68, none over 0.9; 5 of 94 chunks had a most likely language outside en,hi. Same picture as CoSHE-Eval `en` (1.1%) and FLEURS English (0%): Indian-accented English read or spoken in one language is almost never taken for Hindi. NOT measured: Kathbath (the official test sets, hi and ur, are prepared in eval/data but the detection run was not done: it must not run during Night 2; information only, to be done afterwards if wanted).
- Carried to Phase 5 (GATES.md row "Language-detection rules"): rules (a) to (d) are compared on transcript WER and script correctness on code-switched sessions; this measurement only says where the first-pass detector is reliable (single-language speech) and where it is not informative (chunks that mix languages).
