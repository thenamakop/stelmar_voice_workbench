Verdict from the harness (eval/run.py, limit 3.0 points, two-sided): en PASS, hi FAIL, ur FAIL.
In both FAILs the ENGINE has the lower error rate. Not re-run, limit not changed; see "Reading the result" and the question it ends with.

Reading the result
- Utterances with identical text on both sides: en 17 of 20 (3 differ by 2 words, rare-word spelling), hi 17 of 20, ur 18 of 20. Only 3 hi and 2 ur utterances differ, and all of them are the two-chunk utterances (longer than 15 s).
- Hindi, the largest one (fleurs-hi 10798430, same chunk boundaries on both sides): whisper-cli's second chunk is the text "??? ??? ???", the engine's is 12 correct Devanagari words. Three hi utterances and one ur utterance of the whisper-cli run contain such "?" text; the engine's run has none. This is the carry-over item 2c: whisper-cli's JSON output splits a multi-byte character across tokens and prints "?", while the engine joins the raw segment bytes before decoding UTF-8. It is a defect of the reference side, not of the engine.
- The remaining differences are chunk-boundary effects: in fleurs-hi 33253568 the boundary is at 11,400 ms (whisper-cli) against 11,295 ms (engine); in fleurs-ur 64116877 at 8,785 ms against 14,260 ms. The engine's chunker and the harness's VAD step cut at different pauses.
- Speed parity and the text effect of chunk boundaries on single clips: docs/evidence/phase-2/speed-parity.txt (within 1% on processing time).
